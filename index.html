About

👋🏻 Hi, I’m Peijia Deng — also known as Diga Deng. I’m a Computer Science undergraduate at the Paul G. Allen School of Computer Science & Engineering, University of Washington.

I’m interested in computer vision, multimodal learning, embodied AI, and robot learning. I’m especially interested in how intelligent systems can build useful representations of the physical world, integrate visual and sensor information, and use those representations to interact with real-world environments.

My projects explore explainable visual perception and sensor-driven interaction, which have motivated me to think more about the connection between perception, physical-world representation, and action. Alongside this work, I’m building DIGO, a platform for exploring clothing and interiors through everyday scenes.

📑Research & Projects

VISION / XAI

Facial Expression Recognition & Explainable AI

Independent replication & extension · 2025–present

Reproducing a facial expression recognition study and investigating whether model explanations align with human-interpretable facial regions.

The pipeline covers 12 CNN architectures with 5-fold cross-validation, SLIC superpixels, and LIME explanations. A follow-up experiment examines alignment with FACS-based facial masks using an IoU evaluation framework.

Reality-Shake

Multimodal brain–computer interaction prototype · 2025

Connecting concentration signals to real-time interaction in Unity. A Python pipeline processes signals from an Arduino-based device and passes them to the game to drive actions and task progression.

GAME DEVELOPMENT / COMPUTER VISION

Hand off My Planet

Collaborative project · Unreal Engine 5.7

A first-person alien adventure controlled through webcam-based hand gestures and head movements. Players explore a planet, cast spells, and battle enemies using natural movements rather than traditional game controls.

The game combines real-time hand and head tracking with gesture-driven combat, including shields, fireballs, shockwaves, and lasers. COSMO, an AI companion, supports optional real-time voice conversations and interactive tutorials.

Biocompatible Brain–Computer Interfaces

Literature review & conceptual research proposal · 2024

Advisor: Guanxiang Heng

Co-authored a literature review on implanted brain–computer interfaces, examining glial responses, electrode–tissue interactions, and long-term neural signal stability.
